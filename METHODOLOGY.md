# Methodology

All parameters below are planning assumptions chosen for transparency. None are validated astronaut or rover mobility measurements, and none establish that terrain is safe or unsafe.

## Coordinates and units

- Longitude and latitude are Mars planetocentric degrees, east positive, in the -180..180 domain, on a simple cylindrical (plate carree) grid. Leaflet's `EPSG:4326` CRS is used purely as a plate carree plotting frame; no Earth ellipsoid is involved.
- Distances use the haversine formula on a sphere of radius 3,396,190 m (the radius stated in the USGS product label). Elevation is metres.
- The grid origin is the north-west corner; row 0 is the northernmost row.

## Slope (derived)

Slope is computed from the elevation grid with Horn's 3x3 method:

```
dz/dx = ((c + 2f + i) - (a + 2d + g)) / (8 * dx)
dz/dy = ((a + 2b + c) - (g + 2h + i)) / (8 * dy)
slope = atan(sqrt(dz/dx^2 + dz/dy^2))
```

`dx` and `dy` are cell sizes in metres for that row: `dy = dLat * R` and `dx = dLon * R * cos(latitude)`. Pixel differences are never used as distances. A cell whose 3x3 neighbourhood contains a missing value, and the outer border ring, has **unknown** slope (NaN). Missing values are not filled or interpolated.

This is a derived quantity, not a NASA slope product. Block-averaging the source DEM to the working cell size smooths relief, so slopes underestimate steepness at the native 200 m scale.

Unit tests check the method against analytic planes, latitude scaling, and missing-data propagation (`src/core/core.test.ts`).

## Terrain classification

| Class | Rule |
|---|---|
| Low | slope < 5 deg |
| Moderate | 5 deg <= slope < 15 deg |
| High | slope >= 15 deg |
| Unknown | slope could not be computed |

These are descriptive bins. Unknown is a distinct class and is never merged into low.

## Route cost model

For a step between two adjacent cells (8-connected), with length `L` metres:

```
known slopes s1, s2:   factor = 1 + (mean(s1, s2) / S0)^2
either slope unknown:  factor = U            (uncertainty penalty)
either slope > Smax:   step excluded
step cost = L * factor
```

Three profiles are always computed:

| Profile | S0 (deg) | Smax (deg) | U |
|---|---|---|---|
| Shortest | effectively infinite (no slope term) | 25 | 1 |
| Balanced | 10 | 25 | 5 |
| Cautious | 5 | 15 | 20 |

"Shortest" still honours its exclusion limit but applies no slope or unknown-terrain penalty, so it measures distance only.

## Pathfinding

A* on the 8-connected grid with a great-circle heuristic. The heuristic is admissible because every factor is at least 1, so each profile's route is cost-optimal under its own parameters. Waypoints are visited in the order listed; each leg is solved independently and the legs are concatenated. If any leg has no path, that profile reports failure.

## Comparing alternatives

Alternatives are the three profiles above, not random variations. To compare them fairly, every route is re-scored with the **Balanced** parameters ("reference cost", in cost-km). The comparison text is generated from those numbers: lowest reference cost, the distance premium paid for it, identical paths, and unknown-slope share. A profile that produces the same cell sequence as an earlier one is marked "same path".

Per route the app also reports distance by terrain class, mean and maximum slope (known cells only), cumulative ascent and descent from the elevation grid, and the unknown-slope share.

## Duration

Duration is `distance / speed` with a speed the user types in. The default of 2 km/h is a placeholder assumption, not a validated figure, and slope does not alter it.

## Scientific points of interest

Points come from `public/data/pois.json`, curated from the literature cited in each record. Each shows what the cited work reports, the instrument or mission, and caveats. Coordinates are approximate and flagged for verification. They can be chosen as route waypoints, and any point within 3 km of a route's cell centres is listed in its summary. There is no scientific-interest ranking.

## Assumptions and limits

- The grid resolution (about 590 m per cell by default) cannot resolve boulders, small craters or dunes that govern real traversability.
- No roughness, rock abundance, soil strength, thermal or illumination data are used.
- Terrain shown while `synthetic: true` is generated and meaningless for the real surface.


### DEM Resolution Sensitivity Validation

MarsCraft uses a working DEM generated by averaging 3×3 blocks of the original USGS HRSC–MOLA blended elevation raster. This reduces the source raster from 1482 × 1482 to 494 × 494 cells.

To assess the effect of this reduction, we independently calculated terrain slopes at both resolutions using the Horn 3×3 method and compared spatially aligned measurements.

**Elevation aggregation verification**

The reconstructed 3×3 block averages matched the production DEM exactly across 244,036 compared elevation cells, with a maximum absolute difference of 0 m.

**Center-to-center slope comparison**

Across 242,064 valid overlapping cells:

- Mean absolute slope difference: 1.107°
- Median absolute slope difference: 0.732°
- 90th-percentile absolute difference: 2.519°
- 95th-percentile absolute difference: 3.454°
- Maximum absolute difference: 18.242°

The mean signed difference was −0.756°, indicating a general tendency toward lower coarse-resolution slope estimates.

**Within-cell slope variability**

The difference between the 90th-percentile and median fine-resolution slope within each 3×3 block was:

- Mean: 1.132°
- Median: 0.819°
- 90th percentile: 2.339°
- 95th percentile: 3.138°
- Maximum: 16.518°

**Terrain classification sensitivity**

Binary threshold disagreements between spatially aligned fine- and coarse-resolution slopes were 9.56% at 5° and 1.71% at 15°.

**Interpretation and limitations**

The results suggest that the working grid preserves broad-scale slope patterns reasonably well, while some local terrain variations are lost during spatial aggregation. The original-resolution raster is a finer representation of the same blended DEM, not independent ground truth. Consequently, this experiment evaluates resolution sensitivity rather than absolute terrain accuracy.

The working resolution is retained for the regional demonstration, with the limitation that narrow or localized terrain features may not be adequately represented. The terrain classifications and route-planning outputs should not be interpreted as certified astronaut mobility or safety predictions.
## Radiation context (UI only)

The Safety panel shows galactic cosmic ray (GCR) figures from Curiosity RAD at Gale crater (Hassler et al. 2014): about **0.21 mGy/day absorbed dose** and about **0.64 mSv/day dose equivalent**. These are quiet-sun order-of-magnitude values for crew awareness. They are **not** Jezero measurements and are **not** operational limits. Do not label the absorbed-dose number as mSv.

Solar particle events (SPEs) can raise dose rates by orders of magnitude for hours to days. MarsCraft does **not** ingest space-weather forecasts or SPE alerts. The UI states that gap explicitly.

These numbers are for crew awareness in a planning tool. They are not medical limits, EVA stay-time rules, or operational constraints.


## EVA budget check

When a mission scenario is loaded, the UI compares walking time (distance ÷ assumed speed) plus reserved science hours to the scenario EVA budget. Over-budget plans are flagged. Speed and science hours are planning assumptions, not certified timelines.

## Slope class vs finer topography

Coarse grid cells (~590 m) average sub-cell relief. Validation against finer data can show a bias toward classifying some truly steeper patches as lower-slope bins. Treat "100% low slope" on this grid as a planning signal on the working DEM, not a guarantee that the true surface is gentle at rover or foot scale. Prefer the Cautious profile and field judgment near scarps and delta fronts.
